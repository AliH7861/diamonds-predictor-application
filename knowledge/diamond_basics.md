# Diamond Buying Knowledge

## Price and size

- Carat measures weight, and price generally rises faster than carat because larger high-quality stones are rarer.
- In this dataset, carat has the strongest simple numeric correlation with price at approximately 0.92.
- Larger diamonds at the same budget usually require a compromise in cut, color, or clarity.
- A predicted price is supporting evidence from historical rows, not a guaranteed market value.

## Cut

- Cut strongly affects brightness, sparkle, and visual performance.
- The dataset cut grades are Fair, Good, Very Good, Premium, and Ideal.
- A well-cut diamond can look more impressive than a heavier diamond with weaker light performance.

## Clarity

- Clarity describes inclusions and blemishes observed during grading.
- The dataset clarity grades are I1, SI2, SI1, VS2, VS1, VVS2, VVS1, and IF.
- VS diamonds have small inclusions that may be difficult to see without magnification.
- Many buyers can choose a visually clean VS diamond without paying the premium for VVS or IF.

## Color

- The dataset uses color grades D through J.
- Differences between neighboring color grades can be subtle.
- A diamond's setting can affect how its body color appears.

## Dataset findings and limitations

- The source dataset has no customer IDs, certification details, fluorescence, symmetry, polish, seller terms, or market date.
- Buyer segments are interpretations of anonymous diamond purchase profiles, not observed customer identities.
- Price, carat, cut, color, clarity, depth, table, and dimensions should be considered together.
- ANN and benchmark predictions do not inspect damage or the physical stone and do not replace professional appraisal.
